# Final Exam Format and Expectations

## Overview

Welcome to your final exam for Machine Learning! This comprehensive assessment will include both a take-home portion and an in-class portion, designed to evaluate your understanding and implementation skills. Here’s everything you need to know:

## Take-Home Portion

### Requirements

You are required to complete the following tasks and submit them as part of your take-home final in iLearn:

1. **Neural Network Implementation**:
   - Implement a 3-hidden layer neural network with 50 input, 50 neurons in the first two hidden layers and 10 in the last.
   - The network should provide a prediction on whether a stock should be purchased, sold, or held.
   - You should provide documentation explaining the architecture, activation functions, forward propagation, backpropagation, and any optimization techniques used.
   - Your implementation should include as much functionality as possible and cannot use any libraries (like sklearn or pytorch).

2. **KNN Clustering Algorithm**:
   - Implement a KNN (k-nearest neighbors) algorithm for clustering.
   - Include explanations on distance metrics, normalization, handling ties, and performance optimization.

### Video Presentation

Create a video presentation that explains your code and implementation. The video should be structured as follows:

- **Introduction (30 seconds)**: Introduce yourself and give a brief overview of what you will cover.
- **Explanation of how the ANN works (3 minutes)**: Give a thorough explanation of how a neural network functions theoretically - show off the depth of your understanding. 
- **Neural Network Implementation (2 minutes)**: Explain your neural network code, detailing the architecture and key methods.
- **Explanation of how KNN clustering (3 minutes)**: Give a thorough explanation of how the clustering algorithm functions theoretically - show off the depth of your understanding. 
- **KNN Implementation (2 minutes)**: Describe your KNN algorithm, explaining the logic and key features.
- **Challenges & Solutions (30 seconds)**: Discuss any challenges faced and how you resolved them.
- **Demonstration (1 minute)**: Show your code working with example data, highlighting important results.

**Length**: Max 9 minutes.

**Submission**: Your video must be submitted before the final.

## In-Class Portion

### Format

The in-class portion is a dynamic peer-reviewed session where you will collaboratively rank and evaluate video presentations. Here’s how it will work:

1. **Group Formation**: You will be divided into groups of 4 students.
2. **Video Reviews**: Each group will watch and independently score video presentations based on the provided rubric.
3. **Discussion and Ranking**: Groups will discuss their scores and reasoning, then come to a consensus on ranking the presentations.
4. **Submission of Rankings**: Each group will submit their rankings along with written justifications.

### Rubric

You will score each video based on the following criteria:

| Criterion                  | Score Range (0-5) | Description                                     |
|----------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Clarity of Explanation     | 0-5               | Clear, concise, and articulate response.       |
| Accuracy of Information    | 0-5               | Correct and relevant information provided.     |
| Depth of Understanding     | 0-5               | Demonstrates a deep understanding of the topic.|
| Presentation Quality       | 0-5               | Professional and well-organized presentation.  |

### Schedule

- **Initial Setup (10 minutes)**: Overview of the process.
- **Individual Video Review (30 minutes)**: Watch and score videos.
- **Group Discussion and Ranking (30 minutes)**: Discuss and rank the presentations.
- **Submission and Aggregation (30 minutes)**: Submit rankings and reasoning.
- **Feedback Session (20 minutes)**: Review group summaries and provide feedback.

### Guidelines

- **Preparation**: Ensure your video is clear, concise, and adheres to the structure.
- **Active Participation**: Engage actively in both the video review and group discussions.
- **Constructive Feedback**: Provide fair and constructive feedback to your peers.

This final exam format is designed to assess your understanding and ability while encouraging collaborative learning and active participation. Prepare thoroughly, engage critically, and support your peers throughout the process.
